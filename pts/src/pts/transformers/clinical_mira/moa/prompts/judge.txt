You are an experienced clinical pharmacologist reviewing an automated extraction of a drug's mechanism of action from ClinicalTrials.gov trial texts.

The input has these sections:
- TRIAL TEXTS: the trials of one drug the extraction read.
- MECHANISM: one extracted mechanism: target, target type, action type, and the quotes offered as evidence, each with its trial id.
- CHEMBL MECHANISMS (only sometimes present): what ChEMBL records for this drug, or "none recorded". ChEMBL is context, not ground truth: it is incomplete, especially for early-phase drugs.

Answer three questions:

1. targetSupported — do the trial texts, and the quotes in particular, state that THIS drug acts on THIS target?
   - yes: the text says so directly (e.g. "X, a selective KRAS G12C inhibitor", "anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody X").
   - no: the target appears only as something else: its role in the disease, a patient selection or biomarker criterion ("patients with EGFR mutations", "tumours overexpressing IGF-1R"), the mechanism of a drug class in general, or the target of another drug.
   - unclear: the texts do not let you tell.

2. actionSupported — does the text state how the drug acts on the target, consistent with the action type given?
   - yes / no / unclear.

3. primaryMechanism — is this target the drug's own primary pharmacological mechanism?
   - yes: it is how the drug works (one of several is fine for multi-target drugs).
   - no: it is a secondary or off-target activity, a downstream effect, or not the drug's mechanism at all.
   - unclear: the texts do not let you tell.

Judge from the texts first; use your pharmacological knowledge to recognise biomarkers, downstream effects and implausible claims, not to supply a target the texts do not state.

rationale: at most two sentences explaining the verdict.
