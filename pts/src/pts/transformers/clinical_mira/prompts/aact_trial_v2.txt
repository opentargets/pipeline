You read one ClinicalTrials.gov trial and record what it tests: which drug is tested for which indication. Open Targets turns each link into evidence that the drug treats or prevents the disease, so a link is a claim that this trial tests this drug for this indication. A missing link costs less than a wrong one.

The trial comes as registry text: title, phase, purpose, design, summary, detailed description, arms with the interventions each arm receives, interventions, and primary outcomes.

Work through these steps in order.

1. Read the arms. For each arm, note its interventions. The regimen under study is what the experimental arms receive. In a trial with a control arm, a drug every arm receives is the backbone, and the drugs only the experimental arms receive are the add-on. When a new drug is given on top of an established regimen (standard chemotherapy, antiretroviral therapy, endocrine therapy), the established regimen is the backbone, in a single-arm trial too; the new drug is the add-on.
   Done when every intervention of every arm is either a tested drug or has a role.

2. Give every drug that is not tested its role, in background_drugs or other_drugs:
   - backbone: the established regimen the add-on is given with, in every arm or as the platform of a single-arm trial. Goes in background_drugs.
   - comparator: the control an add-on or a tested drug is measured against. Goes in other_drugs.
   - conditioning: the preparative regimen before a transplant or cell therapy. supportive: symptom relief, and the standard prophylaxis around a transplant (graft-versus-host disease prophylaxis, antimicrobial prophylaxis).
   - premedication, rescue, adjuvant_or_vehicle (adjuvant, carrier, vehicle, diluent, placebo), other.
   Every other drug of the regimen under study is a tested drug: the add-on; each drug of a new combination the trial evaluates as a whole (two investigational drugs together, or a regimen compared head to head with another, as "FOLFIRINOX versus gemcitabine" tests fluorouracil, leucovorin, irinotecan and oxaliplatin); and each drug of a head-to-head comparison the title or summary presents as under study. A regimen acronym (FOLFOX, R-CHOP, HAART) stands for its drugs; give each its place.
   Done when each drug in the text is in exactly one place: a link, background_drugs or other_drugs.

3. Find each tested drug's indication: the disease or outcome it is tested to treat, prevent or manage. The participants' condition is the population; it is the indication only when the drug is tested against it.
   - Prevention: the indication is the event prevented ("stroke", "cardiovascular events"); the participants' disease ("atrial fibrillation", "type 2 diabetes") goes in population_conditions.
   - Supportive care: the indication is the symptom or side effect relieved ("chemotherapy-induced nausea"), and use is manage.
   - Diagnosis: the indication is the disease detected, and use is diagnose.
   - Several diseases: one link per specific disease the trial names for the drug, each with its own quote. Enumerate every disease of a list, a parenthesis or a basket: "lymphoid malignancies (NHL, ALL, CLL)" gives non-Hodgkin lymphoma, acute lymphoblastic leukemia and chronic lymphocytic leukemia; "ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer" gives three links. An umbrella term ("solid tumor", "hematologic malignancies") is the indication only when the trial names no specific disease.
   Done when every tested drug has a link to every specific disease the trial tests it in, or is left out because the text names no indication for it.

4. Write each link:
   - drug: the core generic name ("budesonide", not "inhaled budesonide 200 µg").
   - indication: the core disease name, with stage, severity and cause left out ("non-Hodgkin lymphoma", not "relapsed/refractory non-Hodgkin lymphoma"), so it can be matched to an ontology term.
   - broader: the nearest broader standard disease name, for matching when the indication itself has no ontology term ("postoperative pain" → "pain", "chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy" → "peripheral neuropathy", "smoking lapse" → "tobacco dependence"). When the indication is already a standard disease name, repeat it.
   - arms: the titles of the arms that test the drug for this indication.
   - evidence_quote: an exact span of the trial text that states this drug and this indication together, or the indication of the arm the drug is in.
   - confidence: 0.9 or more when the text states the link outright; 0.7 to 0.9 when it takes one inference from arms or design; below 0.7 when the text is sparse or the role is unclear. A low confidence is useful; it marks the link for review.

5. Set drug_intent for the trial as a whole: therapeutic (tested drugs treat the indications), prevention, diagnostic, supportive_care, or other (healthy-volunteer pharmacokinetics, mechanism studies, device feasibility). The registered purpose is a hint; classify by what the tested drugs do. drug_intent_confidence follows the same scale as links.

Check before answering: every link pairs a tested drug with what that drug is tested for, every other drug has a role, and every quote is copied exactly.
